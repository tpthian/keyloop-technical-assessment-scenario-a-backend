# ADR-007: No synchronous CRM dependency; the scheduler is an intake point

Status: Accepted, 2026-11-03 (D-014, D-032, D-036). Supersedes the open item in D-010 (validate IDs against the CRM synchronously, or trust the caller).

## Context
Business stance: never turn a customer away over data quality; the CRM reconciles later.

## Decision
Booking never calls the CRM. The request carries `customer {id?, email?, name?}` and `vehicle {id?, vin?, make?, model?}`, each with at least one identifier, stored as a snapshot on the appointment. An identifier means: vehicle `id` or `vin`; customer `id` or `email`. Name, make and model alone are rejected with 422. The appointment stores a NOT NULL `vehicle_key` derived from the identifier, the `vin` when given, else `id:<vehicle id>`, which `no_vehicle_overlap` constrains (ADR-004).

- Rule: the VIN is normalised to upper case with whitespace removed before `vehicle_key` is derived from it (D-036).
- Limitation: a vehicle booked once by VIN and once by vehicle `id` gets two `vehicle_key` values, so `no_vehicle_overlap` does not catch that overlap.

The CRM receives the `AppointmentConfirmed` event (documented, not built; a transactional outbox is future work) and reconciles. The CRM port shrinks to that outbound event.

## Alternatives rejected
- Synchronous validation, fail-closed: couples booking availability to CRM availability and rejects customers over data quality.
- Trust the caller with IDs only: breaks for new customers, whom the scheduler would have to refuse.

## Consequences
Trade-off accepted: duplicate or unclean customer records in the CRM, in exchange for revenue, retention and one fewer dependency on the booking path. D-010's snapshot (customer_id, vehicle_id, VIN) is widened to the fields above.
